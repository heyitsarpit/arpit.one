- The problem of AI-generated / slop code.
- **“I’ve started closing my eyes a lot more.”**
- The apparent **failure of AI coding capabilities to translate straightforwardly into productivity gains**.
- The thesis on that one is that I trusted the hype too much and I expected that software engineering had been solved.
- What I misunderstood was that coding, specific coding tasks had been mostly solved, where a well-defined task can be solved by a problem, but that can be solved by an AI.
- And while I was working on specific small code bases and on projects where I wanted to achieve a certain result and did not care about the code at all, things did not matter.
- But as soon as complexity grew and I understood that even smarter models were not good enough at making the software better, I understood that software engineering itself is something different from and something bigger than just coding itself.
- And there is a layer of abstraction on top, which the models are not able to ascertain correctly, and they don’t have, like, value judgment.
- They don’t understand what is good or what is bad.
- the problem with slop code is it is optimised to solve the current task at hand with what ever syntax and hacks necessry and big labs have leaned into the hype of oneshotting solutions to prijects and i am never impressed by it because to me it is a reflection of memorised intelligence and not reasoning capability but software engineering is an inhererently iterative discipline and you need to pla long term, reason through consequences of your decisons and build abstractiosn and structures that can be expanded and built up and composed together into a solution and AIs don’t do that.
- how do interact with models in a manner that maximally helps them reason through a problem.
