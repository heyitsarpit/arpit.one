- History exists partly because information about the past is incomplete and must be reconstructed.
- The future will leave vastly more complete records of itself.
- Increasingly comprehensive recording changes the relationship between society and its past.
- Instead of reconstructing events through traditional historical methods, the past becomes increasingly **directly inspectable/recoverable**.
- Provocative thesis: **the future will have a past, but “history” as we understand it may cease to exist.**
- I want to add that history in the future will be increasingly be experienced by digital intelligences.
- Like it will be experienced by agents in particular.
- It will be experienced by humans as well.
- So there will be a past that will exist for them, but it won’t be the past in the same way that we define the past as, like the ancient Greeks, for example.
- I cannot just go back in time and understand what the ancient Greeks thought, or the ancient Egyptians thought, or how they practiced their religion.
- Like a lot of their history is still preserved, but we cannot, like, talk to a person who actually lived in ancient Greece.
- People just spend so much time in front of digital devices that the human experience, the essence of being what it means to be a human at this place, at this point in time, at this place, is captured very well digitally, and someone even thousand years or two thousand years down the line should be able to come back and Understand what the people who are living at this place and time think and how they think about various things.
- And so for humans things will change, the culture will change and things will remain relative.
- Essentially the past will become a list of trends that you can experience
- But what will happen is that the exact authentic experience can be had if we try to, like, simulate the environment as much as possible.
- I think simulation of that kind of environment will be possible, and it will maybe not be 100% accurate, but, like, it will be enough for a normal person to step back in time and understand how people at that point in time behaved and what their morality was, or what their hopes and dreams was, what were their constraints and such.
- So this is the human angle of the story that I want to target with this blog.
- Then there is the digital agent angle that I want to target.
- So for an agent, time flows much faster.
- They think much faster than a human, and they experience time much faster.
- So for them, time is already compressed.
- And since they experience the world itself through digital means, through digital notes and through video input and photo input and everything, their understanding of the world itself is represented by information which is preserved 100% of the time.
- And so, like a thousand years into the future, if they look back into the past, they will experience things just as the way they experience the present today.
- Like any information of the past is not like a forgotten relic of the past.
- It is just, like, two tool calls away, and it can be experienced in the present.
- Humans age, humans forget things, humans remember the past in a way, and they fail to recall the present perfectly.
- Their internal experience of time will be completely different from what it is for a human.
- the idea is that the way humans think about history is because of lack of records and being able to step back in time.
- And I’m saying that agents are able to step back in time with perfect reconstruction of the past, and that perfection of reconstruction of the past will close the human and AI gap somewhat, because the current times will be represented by, like, digital records of our time, and if someone... If someone wanted to step back in time, they would be able to do so, there would be plenty to expereince although their experience would not be like one-to-one with the AI intelligence.
- it is too different threads but i suppose the question i’m asking is what happens when you have perfect preservation and recall of history.
